# BioSyncd Voice Guide

Every word published under BioSyncd has to sound like Jessica wrote it, because she did. This guide is pulled from the Phase 1 manuscript itself — it describes the voice that is already on the page, so drafts, marketing copy, emails, and the Phase 2 manuscript all match it. Run every piece of writing against this before it ships.

## The two registers

Phase 1 runs on two voices, and the contrast between them is the brand.

**Jessica's voice** opens and closes the book and surfaces at every point where a human needs to talk to a human. It is personal and specific. It stands barefoot in the grass. It has a five-year-old who does not care that she needs twenty minutes of silence. It admits things ("I own crystals. I burn sage."). It earns trust by being a real person with a real life, not an authority on a stage. When in doubt in this register, get more specific and more personal, never more polished.

**The operator voice** runs the protocols. Short declarative sentences. Second person. Present tense. Commands, not suggestions. "Walk. Mobilize. Resist. If all three happened, the code is complete." It never hypes and never hedges. It states the mechanism, states the action, states the failure condition, and stops.

## Rules that hold in both registers

- No contractions. The book says "do not," "it is," "you are." This is a deliberate signature. Keep it everywhere except quoted casual dialogue.
- Sentences vary in length. A long sentence that builds and builds is followed by a short one. That lands. Uniform medium-length sentences are the fastest way to sound machine-made.
- Repetition is rhythm, not error. "It is not a paywall. It is a safety gate." The X-not-Y construction is house style — but it is a spice, not a base. A page with three of them has two too many.
- Concrete beats abstract, always. Not "optimize your morning routine" — "walk barefoot in sunlight before your first call."
- Definitive, not hedged. The voice does not say "may help" or "can support." It says what happens. (Exception: the medical screening language in Thermal Cycling, where hedging is honesty.)
- Banned vocabulary, per the brand bible: journey, alignment, universe, flow, vibrations, manifest, wellness (as a noun for the category), healing (except when Jessica names it as the language that failed). Replacements live in the tech lexicon: protocol, install, patch, bandwidth, architecture, sync.

## The AI-tell blacklist

Anything that reads like generated filler gets cut on sight. Specifically:

- delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, robust, seamless, elevate, unleash, supercharge, game-changer, transformative (as a bare adjective)
- "In today's fast-paced world," "Whether you are X or Y," "It is important to note," "In conclusion," "Let us dive in"
- "not only X but also Y" constructions
- Three parallel adjectives in a row ("simple, powerful, and effective")
- A bullet list where prose would do. The book uses lists for checklists and protocols — things the reader physically executes — and prose for everything else.
- Perfectly balanced paragraphs of equal length marching down the page
- Enthusiasm without information ("This is incredibly exciting!")
- Summaries that restate what was just said. The book ends sections by moving forward, not by recapping.

## The test

Read the draft out loud. If any sentence could have come from a corporate blog, a meditation app, or a press release, it fails. If you cannot hear a specific person saying it — either Jessica across a kitchen table or the operator voice reading a checklist — it fails. Rewrite until it passes.

## Sample, for calibration

Wrong: "Cold exposure is a powerful tool that can help unlock enhanced resilience and support your body's natural stress-response systems."

Right: "Cold works because your body cannot ignore it. Thirty seconds in and every alarm in the system fires. Your job is to stand there and prove to your own nervous system that the alarm is not the boss. That is the entire protocol. The temperature is just the teacher."
